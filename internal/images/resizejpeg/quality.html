How to read this report

Reference A: decode the JPEG at full resolution with Go’s standard JPEG decoder, then resize with Lanczos3 to the exact output dimensions listed. These PNGs avoid a second lossy JPEG encode.

Candidate B: fixed-factor candidates in direct rows use reduced IDCT at the stated factor without a subsequent resize. Original thumbnail candidates use reduced decoding followed by Lanczos3. The aggressive strategy chooses a larger reduction; the 2×-headroom strategy retains more pixels before the final resize. If both strategies choose the same factor, a duplicate candidate may be omitted. The selector shows the actual factor used.

Default · 4× headroom + Lanczos3: every row also includes pure-Go reduced IDCT plus Lanczos3, selecting a power-of-two factor that retains at least 4× the target in both axes where the source permits. This commonly leaves 4–8× headroom, with phase-aware filtering for odd source dimensions. It does less work when reduced decoding is selected, but is not pixel-equivalent to full decode plus Lanczos3. Source-limited headroom and buffered progressive/scan handling are labeled.

Options · headroom versus filter: thumbnail rows append 2× Lanczos3 to isolate the headroom change, 4× CatmullRom and 4× Bilinear to isolate the filter change, and 2× Bilinear to combine them. All three filters widen their footprint when downsampling. Less headroom can discard more detail; the shorter filters can change sharpness and ringing. Different-filter errors are still measured against Lanczos3, not against a same-filter reference. The selector shows each actual IDCT factor and whether headroom is source-limited.

When 2× means fewer pixels: moving from 4× to 2× headroom quarters the intermediate pixel count only when the selected IDCT factor doubles in both axes. The factor-8 cap and source dimensions can prevent that; identical factors do not save reconstruction work. This report does not measure CPU time: use the separate options benchmark to evaluate the quality/CPU trade-off.

Inspecting pixels: 1× does not fit the image into the panel; large outputs intentionally overflow. Drag or scroll to inspect synchronized crops. 2× and 4× enlarge with nearest-neighbor rendering to expose individual output pixels. Fit only shrinks when necessary and can hide small differences. CSS pixels are affected by your browser zoom and display scale. The PNG links also allow independent inspection.

Reading the numbers: MAE is mean absolute channel error, RMSE is root mean square channel error, and max is the largest channel error, on the 0–255 RGB scale. Smaller errors and higher PSNR indicate closer agreement with the reference. A PSNR of +∞ means identical pixels. Metrics use the unamplified images; the difference preview multiplies absolute per-channel differences by eight and clips them at 255.

Scope: this is a visual comparison, not a speed benchmark or a comprehensive perceptual test. Progressive checks include nondefault Bilinear and CatmullRom outputs from both APIs; full-IDCT results are also checked against independent same-filter references. They count decoder checks, not human quality ratings. Source attribution below the selectors describes the selected input; input dimensions distinguish the original source from the JPEG used here.
